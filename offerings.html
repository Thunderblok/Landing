The starting point

We do not begin with a chatbot pitch or a generic automation menu. We begin with the outcome, the accountable person, the boundaries, and the evidence the work needs to leave behind.

Ways to work together

One problem.
One working system.

Engagements start small enough to become useful, then earn the right to grow. The exact tools, integrations, and controls are scoped around the work in front of us.

01

Operational intelligence sprint

Turn a vague “we should use AI here” into a clear operating design: where work begins, where judgment belongs, which systems matter, and what a useful first result looks like.

  • Workflow mapping
  • Human decision points
  • First-slice design

02

ThunderBlock pilot

Give one team a shared place to direct human and agent work around a bounded objective. The pilot makes roles, context, review points, and operating history visible together.

  • Shared operating context
  • Role-aware collaboration
  • Operator visibility

03

Governed workflow build

Connect the tools and people already doing the work. We build a bounded workflow around the approvals, handoffs, and records that make the result usable in the real world.

  • System integration
  • Human review paths
  • Evidence at the seams

A practical first engagement

The work earns its way into the system.

We make the next useful move visible before expanding the scope. That keeps the process legible for the people responsible for the outcome.

  1. 01

    Name the operating problemStart with the friction, decision, or handoff that is costing the team time or clarity.

  2. 02

    Set the human owner and boundariesDecide what can be prepared, what must be reviewed, and what must never happen without a person.

  3. 03

    Build the smallest useful loopMake one workflow work end to end before expanding its reach.

  4. 04

    Carry forward what the team learnsKeep the context, decisions, and next actions available for the work that follows.

A good fit

Work that crosses people, tools, and consequences.

  • Lead intelligence that needs human-reviewed follow-through
  • Research, evidence, or document work that must stay coherent over time
  • Customer, field, or project operations with meaningful handoffs
  • Teams that need shared memory without surrendering human direction
