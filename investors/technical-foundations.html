Section 0 — Ontology gate

Before any formal-methods or hardware claim is discussed, three graphs must stay distinct. Collapsing them is the single most common way a diligence reader over-credits this program, and it is the load-bearing correction HC applied to the prior draft:

Evidence and lineage graph:
    append-only DAG
    no behavioral recurrence

Behavior transition graph:
    may contain cycles
    subject to SCC (strongly-connected-component) analysis

Finite observable automaton:
    permitted only after a finite-memory
    time-Markovizability gate

The evidence/lineage graph is what Thunderline actually writes today: ActionReceipts, Vine lineage edges, and the receipt frontier. It is append-only by construction and never loops back on itself — a receipt cannot un-happen. The behavior transition graph is a different object: the state machine a running system traces out, which can and does contain cycles (retries, wakeups, oscillatory hardware modes). The finite observable ω-automaton is a third, stricter object again — a system is only entitled to be described as a finite observable automaton after it has passed a gate proving its observation contract is time-Markovizable (i.e., that a bounded observation window is sufficient to characterize its recurrent behavior).

The failure mode this gate exists to block: describing a system that merely emits an append-only evidence trail as if it were “proven” to be a finite observable automaton. Those are different claims with different proof obligations, and nothing in Thunderline’s receipt spine — before or after this revision — collapses one into the other. Every claim below is scoped to tell you, explicitly, which of the three graphs it is talking about.

Section 1 — Load-bearing preprint (preprint-status caveat preserved)

The strongest single formal-methods anchor available to this appendix is Zhang & Ma (Chrono AI, November 2025), the EBOC (Event-Bounded Observable Construction) paper. It proposes a Büchi-style construction for observable leaf languages under time-Markovizability — in plain terms, a recipe for building a finite automaton over what a bounded external observer can actually see of a system’s recurrent behavior, rather than over the system’s full internal state graph.

Four structural analogies (A0–A3) motivated using this preprint as an anchor rather than a decoration:

  • A0 — bounded observation window. EBOC’s premise that a finite window suffices to characterize recurrence maps onto Thunderline’s tick-bounded execution model (bounded ticks, bounded burst windows) rather than unbounded free-running computation.
  • A1 — leaf-language framing. EBOC’s “observable leaf language” separates what is internally reachable from what is externally observable — structurally the same separation Section 0’s three-graph gate enforces between the behavior transition graph and the finite observable automaton.
  • A2 — Büchi acceptance under a gate, not by default. EBOC does not assert every system qualifies; it defines a gate condition (time-Markovizability) that must be checked. This is the same posture Thunderline takes toward calibration and authority: nothing is admitted by default.
  • A3 — construction, not existence proof for our system. EBOC proves a construction exists if the gate holds; it does not itself prove Thunderline’s runtime satisfies that gate. That verification remains ours to do, not something inherited by citation.

The preprint carries an internal technical note (referred to here, following the prior appendix’s convention, as T13) flagging that its Büchi construction’s complexity bound is stated for the idealized leaf-language case and has not been independently re-derived outside the authors’ own proof. We preserve that flag rather than smoothing it over.

Preprint-status caveat (unchanged from v2, restated because it governs how the whole section may be cited): EBOC is a Research Square preprint dated November 26, 2025. It has not, to our knowledge, cleared peer review. It should motivate a gate-and-adversarial-proof review internally — it must not be cited externally, in a diligence deck or otherwise, as proof that Thunderline “already has” finite observable authority. The correct sentence is “a preprint proposes a construction we intend to gate our own claims against,” not “a paper proves our automaton.”

Rule-110 posture (unchanged): Rule-110 cellular automata are cited only as the standard evidentiary example that simple recurrent rule systems can already reach computational-universality-adjacent expressive power — i.e., as a caution against assuming a small rule table implies a small or tame behavior transition graph. It is not claimed that any Thunderline CA rule is Rule-110-equivalent, Turing-complete, or has been proven decidable or undecidable in any direction.

Section 3 — What is supported and what remains ours

Supported by the cited literature: the existence of formal machinery — SCC classification and composition, entropy-style complexity metrics over automatic sets, tight complementation, and exact polyhedral region representation — that a future finite-observable-automaton layer over Thunderline’s behavior transition graph could, in principle, be built from.

Ours, and ours alone: the specific claim that Thunderline’s evidence/lineage graph, behavior transition graph, and any future finite observable automaton are the right three objects to keep separate for a governed AI substrate; the mapping of that three-graph gate onto ActionReceipts, Vine lineage, and tick-bounded execution; the decision to require a time-Markovizability gate before any subsystem is described as a finite automaton; and — as of this revision — the decision to extend the same “explicit program and evidence boundary before authority” discipline into a physical hardware research lane (Section 4). None of the cited papers make claims about Thunderline’s runtime, about hardware, or about AI governance. The formal support is for the shape of the argument; the argument that this shape is the correct one for our system is a claim we make and must defend on our own evidence.

Section 4 — Physical foundations: the FPOC hardware research lane

4.1 What HC commissioned

HC delivered a full technical research paper — HC-commissioned research, 2026-08-04, docs_and_research_only authority — commissioning a Field-Programmed Operator Cell (FPOC) research direction: a polymorphic CMOS and analog transistor fabric in which the same physical transistor cluster can be placed into one of six operating modes — digital, threshold, transconductance, accumulate, stateful, oscillatory — under program control, rather than being permanently committed to exactly one of them at fabrication time. The paper is a research and design-envelope document, not a fabrication order: no wafer has been run, no MPW slot reserved, no PDK contract signed.

4.2 The load-bearing doctrinal anchor

The sentence that decides whether this hardware lane is even compatible with Thunderline’s existing doctrine, quoted verbatim from HC’s paper:

“The FPOC research lane also fits the uploaded OKO architecture only if the device remains a replaceable executor beneath an explicit program and evidence boundary.”

This is not a stylistic flourish; it is the gate. It means the FPOC is not being architected as a general-purpose accelerator whose answers are trusted because they came off a fast chip. It is a governed executor that receives a typed program, runs it within calibrated limits, and returns bounded evidence — the same posture every other Thunderline governed action already holds itself to under PolicyGate and the receipt spine. A device that failed to be “replaceable” or that produced authority instead of evidence would not fit this architecture, no matter how good its silicon.

4.3 The FPOC receipt model — the receipt spine extends into silicon (structural analogy)

This is the load-bearing structural analogy this revision adds: ActionReceipt’s 17-field sealed discipline (actor, action_id, inputs_hash, status, and the rest — see Thunderline.Thunderbolt.ExecutionRecorder.@seal_fields) extends coherently in shape into physical hardware, because the FPOC receipt model HC specified requires binding an analogous, larger set of fields: device_id + die_id + tile_id, program_hash, coefficient_hash, calibration_hash, mode_schedule, a supply/temperature/monitor summary, ADC config, raw_measurement_hash, quantized output, an error bound, fault/saturation/clipping flags, firmware_hash, compiler_hash, a nonce, DICE attestation, and a signature.

This is a structural analogy, not a proof of hardware. We are observing that the same discipline — bind the claim to the exact conditions that made it meaningful, seal that binding, and treat anything outside the seal as non-authoritative — generalizes from software receipts to physical measurement receipts. We are not claiming any hardware-attested receipt has been produced, verified, or even implemented.

HC’s paper states the reason this discipline is non-optional for analog hardware, verbatim:

“An analog result cannot be ‘proved’ by hashing the output alone. The receipt must bind the program and the conditions under which the physical measurement was meaningful.”

That is the discipline moving from bits to volts and back: a digital result is either bit-exact or wrong, but an analog result is only meaningful relative to a declared program, calibration state, and environmental envelope — hash the output alone and you have thrown away the only thing that made the number trustworthy.

HC’s paper defines four proof classes for exactly this reason:

  • Exact digital — Boolean or integer result; acceptance rule is bit-exact equality.
  • Bounded analog — numeric operator result; acceptance rule is |y − y_ref| ≤ ε under a declared PVT (process/voltage/temperature) envelope.
  • Statistical analog — noisy or stochastic operator result; acceptance rule is a distribution, confidence interval, and repeated-trial contract.
  • Phase / dynamical — oscillator network trajectory or settled state; acceptance rule is phase, energy, convergence, and timeout criteria.

This taxonomy is what the software-side receipt spine looks like when it reaches physical hardware — it exists to prevent an analog result from acquiring authority merely because a physical current produced it.

4.4 The claim ladder (A→E)

HC’s paper frames the entire FPOC program as a sequence of increasingly ambitious, individually falsifiable claims, reproduced verbatim:

Claim A: A transistor cluster can safely alternate between exact digital and analog modes.

Claim B: The shared cluster offers useful density, energy, or latency reuse.

Claim C: Back-gate or retained state improves that advantage.

Claim D: Large tiles remain calibratable, secure, and reproducible.

Claim E: A compiler and receipt system make the substrate generally usable.

HC’s verdict on each, verbatim: “Claim A is strongly plausible and buildable now. Claim B is the decisive architectural court. Claim C has promising device evidence, particularly in FD-SOI and FeFET technologies. Claims D and E are where most analog-computing visions get mugged by converters, variation, routing, calibration, and hand-tuned demos.”

We lean on this laddering deliberately: it means the FPOC program is falsifiable at every phase, not just at the end. A failed Claim B does not let the program quietly pretend Claim C was ever the point — the honest outcome of a Claim B failure is that the program stops or narrows, not that the pitch deck skips to Claim C’s device evidence as if B had been satisfied.

Dates and budgets are planning envelopes, not fabrication commitments. No MPW slot is reserved. No foundry PDK contract exists. Nothing on this roadmap has been executed.

4.6 Recommended initial FPOC

HC’s final strategic recommendation, quoted verbatim:

“A dual-path, differential, standard-CMOS operator island with SRAM-controlled mode switches, volatile programmable bias, current/charge accumulation, comparator and shared-SAR readout, and a configurable ring-oscillator subnetwork — implemented first in 65 nm, then translated to 28 nm FD-SOI with optional FeFET retention only after measured architectural advantage.”

HC’s own framing of why this specific design was chosen over more ambitious alternatives: it is “modest enough to fabricate, rich enough to test the thesis, and strict enough that a successful result would mean something.” That is also this appendix’s posture toward the whole FPOC lane — a deliberately narrow first bet, not a moonshot pitch.

Section 5 — Explicitly not claimed

Carried forward from v2, unchanged:

  • No claim of formal verification, decidability proof, or completeness for any Thunderline runtime component.
  • No claim that any cited automata-theory paper describes, models, or was written about Thunderline.
  • No claim that the EBOC preprint’s construction has been independently re-derived, peer-reviewed, or verified against Thunderline’s actual runtime.
  • No claim that any Thunderline CA rule has been proven Turing-complete, undecidable, or Rule-110-equivalent.
  • No regulatory-compliance or safety-certification claim attaches to any of the above.

New for v3, specific to the FPOC hardware lane:

  • No FPOC silicon exists. The FPOC is a research paper. No wafer, no MPW slot, no PDK contract, no foundry partnership.
  • The FPOC energy/precision numbers in HC’s paper are program targets, not measured values. “Below 1 pJ/MAC at 4-bit” is what a successful 65 nm chip would need to demonstrate to hit the roadmap’s promotion criterion — it is not something any chip has demonstrated.
  • The FPOC “receipt model extends into silicon” story is a structural analogy, not a proof of hardware. We are saying the receipt discipline can extend coherently in shape from software to physical measurement; we are not saying we have produced, sealed, or verified a hardware-attested receipt.
  • The recommended device partition (Generation A / B / C: standard MOS + volatile bias + SRAM; FD-SOI back gate + optional FeFET state; BEOL NVM or heterogeneous 2D/ionic device) is HC’s staging recommendation, not a committed technology plan. Manufacturability, foundry access, PDK availability, and partner queues will change the actual technology mix long before any of this reaches silicon.

Section 6 — Coherent story we can lean behind

HC’s three story lines, verbatim:

“Omega5 has unusually strong artifact custody and fail-closed test discipline. Its next challenge is no longer proving that one packet can be reconstructed; it is converting that discipline into continuously enforced release engineering and closing a small number of authority-critical runtime gaps.”

“Thunderline governs who and what may act. Omega5 makes the machine, program, state and evidence behind that action explicit and reproducible. The current Round 16 package proves request custody and fail-closed execution binding; execution and production authority remain separate future decisions.”

“Omega5 is evolving toward a backend-portable automata substrate in which recurrent behavior can be decomposed, locally judged and compositionally accepted. That SCC-atlas layer is a funded research opportunity, not a currently shipped claim.”

These three lines remain the honest through-line: strong custody discipline today, an explicit separation between what governs and what is governed, and a formal-methods research lane that is funded and directed but not yet shipped. The FPOC hardware lane added in this revision is a fourth instance of exactly the same pattern — a governed research direction under an explicit “evidence before authority” boundary, not a claim of present capability.
